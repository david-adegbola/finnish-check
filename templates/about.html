About this tool

The problem

In Metropolia's internationalisation survey (954 student respondents, results published 6 May 2026), international students said their number one concern is employment. They worry about the labour market and about whether their Finnish is good enough, and they asked for more support with job seeking (Metropolia news, 6.5.2026). Every degree includes a practical training period, so every student has to find a placement.

Many internship ads mention Finnish, but it isn't always clear whether it is required or only an advantage. Students translate ads one by one, or skip Finnish ads entirely and miss ones they could apply to.

How it works

  1. The ads are split (a line of ---, or one CSV row each) and checked against the limits.
  2. Each ad is sent to a language model with a short, fixed instruction (in core/prompts/finnish_level.md). It must answer in a fixed JSON shape: a level, the exact sentence(s) from the ad, and one sentence in English.
  3. The code checks every quoted sentence really appears in the ad. If one doesn't, the answer becomes “unclear”.
  4. The ads are ranked: Finnish not needed, an advantage, unclear, required; within each group by how many of the student's skills the ad mentions (Finnish case endings are handled).

Limitations

  • The labelled set is small and most real ads are public-sector ads that require Finnish; “an advantage” and “not needed” cases are mostly invented examples.
  • The quote check proves the sentence is in the ad, not that the level is right: a real sentence can still be misread. Always read the ad before applying.
  • Skill matching is word matching: “data analysis” won't match “analysing data” in every form.

Privacy

Nothing is stored. Without an API key nothing leaves the server. With a key, the ad text is sent to the Claude API (Anthropic) to be classified; skills are only used on the server, for ranking.
